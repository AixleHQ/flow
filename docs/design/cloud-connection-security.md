# Cloud Connection Security — notes for a reviewing security team

**Audience:** the security or cloud team of an organisation whose developers want Aixle
to run Claude Code against the organisation's **own** Amazon Bedrock account.
**Companion:** `docs/research/technical-aws-bedrock-cloud-provider-auth-2026-07-25.md`
(the engineering design). This page is the part worth handing to a reviewer.

---

## 1. What the grant does and does not allow

Whichever path you choose, Aixle ends up able to do exactly one thing: **invoke Bedrock
models in your account**, on behalf of the developer who connected.

Granted:

- `bedrock:InvokeModel`, `bedrock:InvokeModelWithResponseStream`
- `bedrock:ListInferenceProfiles`, `bedrock:GetInferenceProfile` (model discovery; without
  the latter every new model costs an extra round-trip while the CLI retries with the
  other request shape)
- `aws-marketplace:ViewSubscriptions`, `aws-marketplace:Subscribe`, conditioned on
  `aws:CalledViaLast = bedrock.amazonaws.com` — this is what lets Bedrock accept a first
  call for an Anthropic model, and it cannot subscribe you to anything else

Not granted, and not requested: reading or writing any other AWS service, creating IAM
principals, changing your Bedrock configuration, or invoking Bedrock outside the inference
profiles you allow. You can narrow the `Resource` list to specific inference-profile ARNs
to restrict which models are reachable.

## 2. Where prompts go

**Prompts and completions travel from the developer's session container directly to
Bedrock.** They do not pass through Aixle's application servers. What crosses our
infrastructure is the credential exchange described below, plus the usual platform
metadata (token counts, session identifiers).

Two exceptions worth naming explicitly:

- If you connect through **your own LLM gateway** (a supported path), prompts go to your
  gateway, which is yours.
- Bedrock's own invocation logging is off by default and, if you enable it, writes to
  **your** account. We never enable it.

Data retention is entirely yours: Bedrock retention modes are configured on your account
or per Bedrock project. Note that Claude Fable/Mythos-class models require the
`provider_data_share` retention mode and show as unavailable without it — that is an AWS
and Anthropic arrangement, not ours.

## 3. The three connect paths, and the trust each creates

### 3a. Cross-account role — recommended

Your admin applies a Terraform module we generate. It creates one IAM role in your
account whose trust policy allows `sts:AssumeRole` from our AWS account **only when the
correct `sts:ExternalId` is presented**. Nothing else is created.

- The ExternalId is generated by us, per customer, and baked into the module body rather
  than exposed as a variable — a value you could silently change is a value that stops
  protecting you. AWS is explicit that the ExternalId is **not a secret**; its job is to
  prevent one Aixle tenant's role from being assumable on behalf of another.
- Before we store your role ARN we run AWS's prescribed confused-deputy check: we attempt
  the assume **without** the ExternalId. If that succeeds, we refuse the connection and do
  not store the ARN at all — because a role assumable without the condition is a role
  anyone else could assume too.
- Sessions are one hour, because role chaining caps `DurationSeconds` at 3600 regardless
  of the role's own `MaxSessionDuration`. Credentials are re-minted per session, never
  cached across them.

Revocation: delete the role, or remove the trust statement. Effect is immediate for new
credential requests, and within the hour for issued ones.

### 3b. IAM Identity Center sign-in — for developers without IAM rights

The developer signs in to **your** Identity Center through the OAuth 2.0 device
authorization grant and approves in their own browser. Nothing is created in your
account; the developer gets exactly the permission set you already assigned them.

**Read this part before approving it.** The device-code grant is a published
credential-phishing technique, and PKCE-by-default in AWS CLI 2.22.0 was AWS's mitigation.
We are aware that this flow resembles the attack, and we do not pretend otherwise:

- What makes the attack work is a stranger getting a victim to approve a code the stranger
  initiated. Here the developer initiates the flow themselves, in your product, for your
  Identity Center.
- PKCE is not usable in our case: its callback must open on the machine running the client,
  and the client runs in a headless container. That is why the device grant is used at all.
- You will see `CreateToken` events in CloudTrail with
  `grantType=urn:ietf:params:oauth:grant-type:device_code`. If you alert on those, expect
  them from developers who connect this way. If your policy is to block the device
  endpoint, use path 3a instead — it needs no browser flow.
- Our connect screen names the Identity Center instance, and shows the account and
  permission set that will be used, before anything is stored.

What we store, encrypted: the OIDC client registration and the refresh token. Identity
Center caps client registrations at **90 days**, after which refresh is impossible and the
developer must sign in again — we cannot extend that. Access tokens are one hour and
refreshed server-side.

Revocation: revoke the developer's Identity Center session or unassign the permission set.

### 3c. Bedrock API key, or your own gateway

A long- or short-term Bedrock API key, or a base URL for a gateway you operate. Nothing is
created in your account and no role is assumed; the value is stored encrypted and injected
into the developer's session.

We flag this as the least preferred path for a reason: a long-term Bedrock API key can be
created with **no expiry at all**, and short-term keys are computed client-side so their
creation is invisible in CloudTrail (their *use* is logged, and both can be constrained
with `bedrock:CallWithBearerToken` / `bedrock:BearerTokenType` — deny both the `bedrock:`
and `bedrock-mantle:` namespaces to close it fully).

## 4. Attribution — what you will see in your own logs

Per-developer, without you having to manage anything:

- **`RoleSessionName`** is the developer's Aixle user id on every assume. It appears in
  your CloudTrail and in Cost and Usage Report 2.0's `line_item_iam_principal`, so
  per-developer Bedrock spend is attributable with zero managed resources.
- **`sts:SourceIdentity`** carries the same identity for cross-account audit.
- Bedrock invocations are ordinary `bedrock:InvokeModel` events in your account, in the
  region you configured.

Ask us for the mapping from Aixle user id to person; it is stable.

## 5. What lives where

| | Stored by us | Stored in your account | In the developer's container |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role ARN + ExternalId (3a) | yes, encrypted; neither is a secret | the role itself | no |
| Identity Center registration + refresh token (3b) | yes, encrypted | nothing | no |
| API key / gateway URL (3c) | yes, encrypted | nothing | yes, in process env |
| Short-lived AWS credentials | no | n/a | yes, ≤1 hour, never written to disk by us |
| Prompts and completions | no | only if you enable Bedrock invocation logging | in the session, as the agent's own working state |

Session containers are ephemeral and hold no persistent credential: a helper asks our
vending endpoint per session, authenticated by a key derived from the session, and that
endpoint refuses to vend for a session that is no longer running.

A connection also belongs to the one company it was made in. A developer who works for
several companies on our platform connects each separately, and a session can only vend the
connection of the company that session is billed to — so your grant is never reachable from
work done for someone else.

## 6. Questions we expect, answered

**Can a developer use this to reach non-Bedrock services in our account?** No. The role
policy grants Bedrock actions only, and the Identity Center path gives them the permission
set you already assigned — no more.

**Can Aixle use the grant when the developer is not working?** Credentials are vended only
for a live session belonging to that developer. A finished session cannot vend.

**What happens if we rotate or revoke?** New credential requests fail immediately;
already-issued credentials expire within the hour. The developer sees the failure before
their next session starts, with the reason, rather than a silently broken agent.

**Can we see what it would cost before committing?** Yes — Bedrock spend is ordinary AWS
billing in your account, draws down your EDP/PPA commitments at full value, and can be
paid with AWS credits. Nothing routes through our billing.
