- (event by coach) Create from a rich, pre-sorted input set: a final architecture spine (18 ADs, reviewer-gate passed), its 50-entry memlog, the companion RFC, and the market research. Direct extraction, no elicitation
- (direction by user) Adopt the architecture spine as a companion so its AD IDs stay citable downstream; do not restate its rules in the kernel
- (capability by coach) CAP-1 (STAGE-0 foundation): a person holds several distinct sign-in identities on one account, and any session they hold can be listed and revoked
- (capability by coach) CAP-2 (ORG-POLICY): a company admin chooses which authentication methods are accepted for entry into their company, without stranding any member
- (capability by coach) CAP-3 (STAGE-1 oidc): a person signs in with a Microsoft work or school account, and a company connects its own OIDC provider
- (capability by coach) CAP-4 (STAGE-2 passwordless): a person signs in without a password - passkey, one-time code, or emailed link - and can add a second factor
- (capability by coach) CAP-5 (STAGE-3 saml): an enterprise customer signs in through their own SAML identity provider
- (capability by coach) CAP-6 (STAGE-4 scim): a customer's directory provisions and deprovisions members of their company automatically
- (constraint by user) Enterprise SSO must work for self-hosted OSS installs; nothing in the login path may require a vendor account
- (constraint by user) No paid external dependency in the login path
- (constraint by coach) Apache-2.0 and publicly redistributed: anything bundled must be redistributable, and anything run in the managed cloud must not create copyleft-as-a-service obligations. This is what eliminates AGPL and source-available identity products
- (constraint by user) Company auth policy is enforced at company entry with step-up re-authentication, never at the login screen
- (constraint by user) At the STAGE-0 cutover every live cookie session is invalidated once; no legacy proof shape is built
- (constraint by user) A passkey is the user's credential: registration, listing and deletion belong to the user alone, while accepting it for entry remains a company policy row
- (constraint by coach) Rails remains the system of record for users, companies, memberships and sessions; the existing login page and organization model must survive
- (constraint by coach) The default self-hosted footprint stays one Rails app and a Postgres; a self-hoster who does not want SAML gains no service to run
- (constraint by coach) Repo rule: every Rails, test and migration command runs inside the web container; the full check suite must be green before a branch is pushed
- (decision by coach) Non-goal: becoming an OIDC provider for customers' own tools. Ory Hydra noted in the research as the low-risk path if it is ever wanted
- (decision by coach) Non-goal: replacing companies/company_memberships with a vendor's organization model. No evaluated product models this domain better
- (decision by coach) Non-goal: adopting Keycloak as the primary IdP. Evaluated on its merits and declined on fit; the broker-only fallback shape is recorded in the RFC section 6
- (decision by coach) Non-goal: additive MFA (SAML AND TOTP) in this scope. The proof model's non-empty-intersection test is deliberately OR-only; upgrading it later is a one-predicate change
- (decision by coach) Non-goal: per-company login-page branding beyond the existing Company#branding
- (decision by coach) Success signal: a customer's IT department connects their own identity provider, restricts their company to it, and a contractor on a foreign email domain cannot get in any other way - while a self-hosted operator reaches the same outcome with containers they run themselves and no vendor account
- (assumption by coach) The Ory Polis OSS build supports enough tenancy for per-company connections. Its README gates 'advanced scaling and multi-tenancy' behind the Ory Enterprise License without drawing the line. Unverified; a half-day spike settles it and it blocks CAP-5 only
- (assumption by coach) Scale is low thousands of monthly active users, B2B. Nothing in the design depends on it, but it is why no sharding or session-store tier is considered
- (question by coach) Does super_admin bypass the entry gate entirely, or only the policy-activation guard? Resolve before CAP-2 ships
- (question by coach) IdP-initiated SSO inverts the 'authenticate, then enter a company' order the entry gate assumes. Enterprise buyers expect it; the sidecar supports it; our gate has not been designed for it. Resolve during the CAP-5 spike
